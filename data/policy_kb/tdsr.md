# Total Debt Servicing Ratio (TDSR)

TDSR limits the proportion of a borrower's gross monthly income that can go towards servicing all debt obligations. It applies to all property loans granted by financial institutions in Singapore.

## Current TDSR Threshold: 55%

A borrower's total monthly debt repayments — including the new property loan being applied for — must not exceed 55% of their gross monthly income.

## What Counts as Debt

All outstanding loan obligations are included in the TDSR calculation:
- Property loans (existing and the new loan being applied for)
- Car loans
- Personal loans and credit lines
- Student loans
- Credit card outstanding balances (counted at 5% of the outstanding balance as a monthly obligation)
- Any other credit facilities

## Income Definition for TDSR

### Employment Income
- Fixed salary: 100% of gross monthly salary
- Variable income (bonuses, commission): 70% of the average over 12 months

### Rental Income
- 70% of gross rental income (after deducting property tax and maintenance)

### Self-Employment / Business Income
- 70% of average monthly income (assessed over 2 years of NOA)

### Investment Income
- Dividends, interest: assessed case by case by lender

## TDSR Calculation Example

Gross monthly salary: SGD 10,000
Existing car loan repayment: SGD 1,000/month
Proposed property loan repayment: SGD 3,500/month
Total debt: SGD 4,500/month
TDSR: 4,500 / 10,000 = 45% → passes TDSR (below 55%)

If property loan were SGD 5,000/month:
TDSR: 6,000 / 10,000 = 60% → fails TDSR (above 55%)

## Stress-Test Rate for TDSR

Financial institutions must apply a minimum stress-test interest rate of 4.0% per annum when computing the monthly repayment for TDSR assessment, even if the actual loan rate is lower. This ensures borrowers can withstand rate increases.

## TDSR and Investment Properties

For investment properties (properties not for owner-occupation), lenders may apply stricter income haircuts at their discretion.

## TDSR Exemptions

Owner-occupiers refinancing existing property loans with no cash-out are exempt from TDSR assessment under certain conditions (MAS Notice 645/1121).

## Relationship with MSR

For HDB flats, the Mortgage Servicing Ratio (MSR) of 30% applies in addition to TDSR. MSR is the binding constraint for HDB purchases since 30% < 55%.

## Historical Context
- Jun 2013: TDSR first introduced at 60%
- Sep 2022: reduced to 55% (tightened to cool the market)
- Jul 2023: current framework confirmed

Always verify effective_date. MAS reviews TDSR periodically.
